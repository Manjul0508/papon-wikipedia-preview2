Angaraag Mahanta, known professionally as Papon, is an Indian playback singer, composer, songwriter and record producer from Assam. He works across Assamese folk and devotional traditions, Indian classical music, film music, pop, rock, electronica, ghazal and Sufi music. His recorded work spans Assamese, Hindi, Bengali, Tamil, Kannada, Marathi, Odia and other Indian languages.[16]

He is the son of Assamese musicians Khagen Mahanta and Archana Mahanta. His national film work includes “Jiyein Kyun”, “Kyon”, “Kaun Mera”, “Moh Moh Ke Dhaage”, “Humnava”, “Bulleya”, “Labon Ka Karobaar”, “Chaav Laaga”, “Nazar Teri Toofan” and songs from Metro... In Dino. Alongside playback singing, he has released independent albums, composed television and event music, worked in film production, appeared on music programmes, and participated in social and environmental initiatives.

Career [edit]

Early recordings and Assamese work

Papon has said that his first official lead recording was the Assamese song “Gaantu Je Gaai Asila” for the 1988 album Aamiu Gao Gaan, recorded as a duet with Dr Lima Das.[2] In the late 1990s and early 2000s he recorded Assamese material including “Nasaba Sokule” with Zubeen Garg and later released his first solo Assamese album, commonly listed as Jonaaki Raati / Junaki Raati.[3]

Hindi-film and national career

A Reuters interview places his Hindi-film chronology before the often-cited 2006 entry: Papon described “Sajna Baware” from Let’s Enjoy (2004) as a track he sang, wrote and produced, later co-produced by MIDIval Punditz. He described “Jiyein Kyun” from Dum Maaro Dum (2011) as the song that gave him his major Bollywood breakthrough.[4]

He subsequently recorded “Kyon” for Barfi!, “Kaun Mera” for Special 26, and in 2015 sang “Moh Moh Ke Dhaage” for Dum Laga Ke Haisha, “Humnava” for Hamari Adhuri Kahani and “Main Tujhse Pyaar Nahin Karta” for Baby, the last composed by M. M. Kreem.[28] “Moh Moh Ke Dhaage” brought him several major awards, including IIFA, Screen, GiMA, Mirchi and TOIFA honours, while it was a Filmfare nomination rather than a Filmfare win.[17][18]

2017–present

In 2017 he was a coach and judge on season 2 of The Voice India Kids.[12] He and Shweta Mishra Mahanta established Earthful Foundation in 2018.[9] In 2022 he attended the Cannes Film Festival as a cultural ambassador of Assam and presented teasers connected with The Land of the Sacred Beats and The Mystical Brahmaputra – A Musical Story.[7][8]

In 2025, Virgin Music Group India announced an exclusive distribution partnership with Papon and described “Room Khali Hai” as the second track from his independent project Pura Asmaan, following “Ab Rukna Mat”.[15] He also recorded multiple songs for Metro... In Dino and appeared onscreen within the film’s recurring musical-performance framework.[14] In 2026 he won the Filmfare Award Assamese for Best Playback Singer (Male) for “Xopun Xopun” from Jiya and was nominated at the News18 Showsha Reel Awards for “Qayde Se” from Metro... In Dino.[19][20]

Production and Cannes projects

During his 2022 Cannes visit as a cultural ambassador of Assam, Papon presented teasers for The Land of the Sacred Beats and The Mystical Brahmaputra – A Musical Story. Independent reporting later described both as projects produced by him.[7][8]

Live ensemble and concert identity [edit]

In 2007 Papon formed the folk-fusion live group Papon and the East India Company, blending Assamese folk and Indian classical influences with rock, blues, trip-hop and electronic elements. He later explained that membership changes meant the East India Company name was no longer used and that the performances had become “Papon’s set”.[5]

Current ticketing and concert listings commonly present his shows as Papon Live, Papon Live in Concert or Papon in Concert. This preview treats those as contemporary event branding rather than asserting that “Papon Live” is a separately constituted band.[42]

International performances

Papon and the East India Company were selected for Celtic Connections 2014 in Glasgow as part of the British Council-linked Folk Nations programme.[38] Earlier international festival appearances include Switzerland’s Paléo Festival in collaborative electronic/fusion settings. Paponists [edit]

Papon’s organised fan community is known as the Paponists. Hindustan Times has reported on the community and socially oriented activities connected with his public messages.[6]

Birthday-related initiatives attributed to fan groups—not to Papon personally—have included blood-donation camps, visits to old-age homes and orphanages, cleanliness activities, tree planting and food distribution. A 2024 report described these as recurring fan-organised activities dating back to around 2010.[44] They are therefore grouped here under the fan community rather than presented as Papon’s own philanthropy.

Philanthropy and environmental work [edit]

Papon and Shweta Mishra Mahanta established Earthful Foundation in 2018. The organisation describes work involving environmental conservation, sustainable livelihoods, health, nutrition and disaster response.[9]

In 2014, Papon and Vishal Dadlani spearheaded the #FORASSAM fundraising concert for people affected by floods in Assam.[11] Relief work involving Papon was again reported during the 2019 Assam floods, and Earthful Foundation later carried out COVID-related work in rural Assam, including the Xokaah initiative.[10]

Editorial choice: vague claims such as “helping the needy” are intentionally omitted. Fan-organised birthday activities are kept in the Paponists section so they are not misrepresented as Papon’s personal charitable work.

Personal life and education [edit]

Papon grew up in a musical household in Assam. After school he moved to Delhi, initially explored architecture and later enrolled in English studies at the University of Delhi, studying at Ramjas College and later Motilal Nehru College before leaving college and pursuing music full-time.[1] He is married to Shweta Mishra Mahanta.
